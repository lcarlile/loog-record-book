The Draft

Nine drafts, 1,464 picks. A pick is worth the points it scored above what that draft slot normally returns at its position — so a late-round quarterback who finishes as the best in the league is worth far more than one taken first. Scoring changed over the years, so points are put on a common scale and every season is centred on zero: each manager is judged against the field he actually drafted against. Keepers taken at a discounted round are excluded from draft grades and scored separately below.

Luck of the Schedule

Every week, compare each manager's score against everyone that week, not just their opponent. That gives the record they deserved. The bar is what they actually got, minus what they earned — gold means the schedule was kind.

Reconciled against ESPN. All 96 season rows in the spreadsheet match the box scores exactly on wins, losses, points for and points against, as do every champion and runner-up. Three fields did not, and use the ESPN figures here: playoff appearances (the sheet undercounted six — six teams make the bracket every year, so the total is 54) and division titles (Karen 3→2, Kaila 2→1). Every last-place finish is as the league recorded it.
Last place means two different things by era, matching how the league actually awarded it. From 2017 to 2021 it went to the worst regular-season record. From 2022 — when the league grew to twelve teams and added a consolation bracket — it goes to whoever finished last in the final standings. 2024 had none recorded and is filled in under the 2022-onward rule.
Seasons finishing exactly .500 are counted as their own category rather than as losses, which moves Karen from four losing seasons to three.
